Teams

Grouping Priya and Ravi so we grant access once, not twice — and every future joiner just gets added to a group.

1. The Production Story

Day 5 at ABC Bank. Priya Nair and Ravi Krishnan now exist as Users inside the ABC-Bank-Cards-Payments Organization, but neither can see a single Job Template yet. Our Team Lead reminds us that three more Cards & Payments engineers are joining over the next two months, and asks a fair question: "Are you planning to click through the same permission screens individually for every single engineer, forever?"

The answer is no — that doesn't scale, and worse, it's exactly the kind of manual, inconsistent access-granting that leads to audit findings ("engineer X had access nobody remembers granting"). Today we create a Team, and from now on we grant access to the Team once — every current and future member automatically inherits it.

2. Why Teams, When Users Already Exist

A Team lives inside an Organization and is simply a named group of Users. Its entire purpose is to be the thing RBAC roles get attached to, instead of attaching a role to five individual people one at a time. Think of it as a distribution list for permissions — add someone to the list, they inherit everything the list already has; remove them, and every one of those permissions disappears in one action.

Without TeamsWith Teams
Grant RBAC role to Priya. Grant same role to Ravi. Repeat for every new joiner.Grant RBAC role to "Cards-Payments-Engineers" Team once.
Offboarding means hunting down every individual grant across every objectOffboarding means removing one person from the Team — done
Easy to forget one grant, causing inconsistent access between teammatesEvery Team member has identical access by construction

3. The Teams Screen

Where: left nav, under Access → Teams. Who uses it: Automation Admins to create Teams and manage membership; Team Leads are sometimes given delegated rights to add/remove their own team's members. What you see: a list of Teams, each tagged with its parent Organization.

5. Hands-On: Creating the Cards-Payments-Engineers Team

  • Click Access → Teams → Add.
  • Name: Cards-Payments-Engineers
  • Organization: select ABC-Bank-Cards-Payments.
  • Description: as above.
  • Click Save. The Team now exists but has zero members and zero access — both still to be added.

6. Adding Members to the Team

  • Open the Cards-Payments-Engineers Team → click the "Users" tab.
  • Click "Associate".
  • Search for and select Priya Nair, then Ravi Krishnan.
  • Click Save. Both are now Team members. Note they still can't launch anything — Team membership alone grants no permission, exactly like Organization membership in Project 04.
Two layers, easy to confuseAn Organization answers "which tenant boundary does this identity live in?" A Team answers "which access group is this identity part of?" Neither, on its own, grants a single permission — that's the job of RBAC role assignment, which we do properly in Project 19.

8. ABC Bank's Team Design (Per Organization)

TeamPurpose
Cards-Payments-EngineersDay-to-day engineers — Execute access on non-production and standard production templates.
Cards-Payments-ApproversSenior engineers/leads — additionally granted Approve rights on Approval Nodes (Project 20) for high-risk changes.
Cards-Payments-AdminsSmall group who can edit the Team's own Templates, Credentials, and Inventories, but not platform-wide settings.

Ravi Krishnan, being senior, is placed in both Cards-Payments-Engineers and Cards-Payments-Approvers — Team membership is many-to-many, a User can belong to more than one Team.

10. Troubleshooting

SymptomLikely Cause
Added a user to a Team but they still see nothingExpected — Team membership grants no access by itself until an RBAC role is attached to the Team (Project 19)
Can't add a user from another Organization to this TeamWorking as designed — Teams cannot cross Organization boundaries
Removed someone from a Team but they can still launch a jobCheck if they were also granted access individually, or belong to a second Team with the same permission — remove all paths, not just one

11. Interview Prep — Say This Out Loud

"What's the difference between an Organization and a Team in AAP?"

Organization is the top-level tenant boundary that everything belongs to. Team is a named group of Users inside one Organization, used purely as the attachment point for RBAC roles, so permissions are granted once to the group instead of repeatedly to individuals.

"Why split engineers into multiple Teams instead of one big Team per department?"

Different roles need different access — a junior engineer shouldn't automatically inherit production-approval rights just because they sit on the same floor as someone who has them. Splitting by responsibility (Engineers / Approvers / Admins) keeps least-privilege intact.

12. Wrap-Up

Key Learnings

  • A Team is a group of Users inside one Organization, and it's the object RBAC roles get attached to.
  • Team membership alone, like Organization membership, grants zero permissions on its own.
  • A User can belong to more than one Team.
