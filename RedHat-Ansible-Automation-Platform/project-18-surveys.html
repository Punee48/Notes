Surveys

Letting an engineer safely tell a playbook "which server" or "how many days" — without ever touching the playbook itself.

1. The Production Story

Day 18 at ABC Bank. Priya needs to run a "clean up old log files" playbook, but the number of days of logs to retain varies by request — sometimes 7 days, sometimes 30. Right now, the only way to change that number is editing the playbook's default variable directly in Git, which means a pull request and a review cycle for something that should be a simple, safe choice at launch time.

Our Team Lead's answer: a Survey — a form that pops up when Priya clicks Launch, asking exactly the questions she needs to answer, with guardrails so she can't accidentally type something dangerous.

2. Why Not Just Let Engineers Edit the Playbook?

Letting engineers freely edit playbook variables at launch time (via "extra variables") is powerful but risky — nothing stops someone from typing an unexpected value that breaks a task, or worse, injecting something malicious. A Survey constrains what can be entered: a dropdown only offers valid choices, a number field enforces min/max, and a required field can't be skipped. The engineer gets flexibility; the platform keeps control.

3. The Survey Tab

Where: inside any Job Template — Resources → Templates → (template name) → Survey tab. Who uses it: Automation Admins build the Survey once; every engineer launching the template answers it. What you see: a list of questions in order, each editable, plus a toggle to enable/disable the Survey entirely without deleting the questions.

6. Reading Survey Answers Inside the Playbook

---
# File: playbooks/log_cleanup.yml
- name: Clean Up Old Log Files
  hosts: linux_servers
  gather_facts: no

  tasks:
    - name: Remove log files older than the requested retention period
      ansible.builtin.find:
        paths: /var/log/cardsapp
        age: "{{ retention_days }}d"  # Reads the Survey answer directly — "d" means days
        recurse: yes
      register: old_logs

    - name: Delete matched files
      ansible.builtin.file:
        path: "{{ item.path }}"
        state: absent
      loop: "{{ old_logs.files }}"

The playbook never hardcodes "7" or "30" — it simply trusts whatever value the Survey collected into retention_days, already guaranteed to be a number between 1 and 90 before the job even starts.

7. Hands-On: Building the Survey

  • Open Resources → Templates → Cards - Log Cleanup → Survey tab → click Add.
  • Question: "How many days of logs should be retained?"
  • Answer Variable Name: retention_days
  • Answer Type: Integer; Default: 7; Minimum: 1; Maximum: 90; Required: Yes.
  • Save the question, then toggle the "Survey Enabled" switch at the top of the tab — a Survey with questions but left disabled will not actually prompt anyone.
  • Launch the template. Priya now sees a small form asking for the retention period before the job starts, instead of ever opening the playbook.
Common mistakeBuilding all the Survey questions correctly, then forgetting to toggle "Survey Enabled" — the questions silently never appear at launch, and the playbook falls back to its own internal default instead.

10. Interview Prep — Say This Out Loud

"Why use a Survey instead of just letting engineers pass extra variables at launch?"

A Survey constrains input — dropdowns only offer valid choices, numeric fields enforce min/max, required fields can't be skipped. Free-form extra variables give no such guardrails, so a Survey gives engineers self-service flexibility while the platform still controls what values are actually possible.

"How does a playbook receive a Survey answer?"

Through the Answer Variable Name configured on the question — the playbook simply references that variable name with normal Jinja2 syntax, with no special Survey-aware code needed.

11. Wrap-Up

Key Learnings

  • Surveys constrain launch-time input instead of relying on free-form extra variables.
  • The Answer Variable Name is the direct link between the form field and the playbook variable.
  • "Survey Enabled" must be toggled on, separately from adding the questions.
