1. The Production Story

Day 4 at ABC Bank. HR has confirmed the Cards & Payments team's first two engineers are joining the automation rollout next Monday — Priya Nair (a mid-level engineer who will build and run playbooks) and Ravi Krishnan (a senior engineer who will also be trusted to approve production-impacting jobs). Our Team Lead asks us to create their AAP accounts inside the ABC-Bank-Cards-Payments Organization we built yesterday, and to make sure Priya absolutely cannot promote herself to admin rights by accident — a control the last audit specifically flagged as missing in the bank's old on-prem tooling.

2. Why Users Aren't Just "Logins"

In most tools, a "user" is just a login. In AAP, a User is the anchor for everything RBAC will later hang off — every permission, every audit-trail entry ("who ran this job") ties back to this object. Getting the User's Organization and type right on day one avoids painful RBAC clean-up later.

3. The Users Screen

Where: left nav, under Access → Users. Who uses it: Automation Admins only. What you see: a table of every user on the platform, with Username, First/Last Name, Email, and a badge showing whether they're a Normal User, Auditor, or System Administrator.

When we use this screenWhenever HR confirms a new joiner or a role change — and just as importantly, the moment someone leaves the team or the bank, so their access is revoked immediately.

4. The Three User Types

TypeWhat they can doABC Bank example
Normal UserCan only do what their assigned RBAC roles (Project 19) explicitly allow — nothing by default.Priya Nair — runs Job Templates she's been granted
AuditorRead-only visibility across everything they can see — every Job, every Template, every Credential's metadata (never the secret itself) — but cannot launch or edit anything.Given to ABC Bank's internal audit team during compliance reviews
System AdministratorFull platform control, including creating Organizations and other admins.Reserved for the two-person Automation Platform team only
Direct answer to our Team Lead's concernPriya is created strictly as a Normal User. Normal Users have no path to grant themselves admin rights — that toggle exists only on the User's own edit screen, which only a System Administrator can reach. This is exactly the self-escalation gap the audit flagged in the old tooling.

6. Hands-On: Onboarding Priya Nair

  • Click Access → Users → Add.
  • Username: priya.nair
  • Email: her ABC Bank email address.
  • First Name / Last Name: Priya / Nair.
  • Organization: select ABC-Bank-Cards-Payments from the dropdown.
  • User Type: leave as Normal User — do not touch the System Administrator toggle.
  • Click Save.
  • Repeat for Ravi Krishnan. We stop here for today — Priya and Ravi exist, but can see nothing yet, because they have no RBAC role or Team membership. We add them to a Team in Project 05, and give them an actual RBAC role in Project 19.
Production tipCreating a User with zero permissions attached is a completely normal, safe intermediate state — it's a good habit to separate "does this identity exist" from "what can this identity do," which is exactly the separation Teams and RBAC give us.

7. Offboarding — Just as Important as Onboarding

When an engineer leaves ABC Bank or moves teams, their AAP account must be disabled the same day — this is a standard audit checkpoint. AAP doesn't let you fully delete a user who has job history (deleting would break the audit trail), so instead we set their account inactive.

Common mistakeDeleting a departed user's account outright. This can break historical Job records that reference them. The correct action is to mark the account inactive and remove their Team/RBAC assignments — the audit trail of what they did while employed stays intact.

10. Interview Prep — Say This Out Loud

"What are the different user types in AAP?"

Normal User (permissions entirely from assigned RBAC roles), Auditor (read-only across everything visible to them), and System Administrator (full platform control). Almost every engineer should be a Normal User — admin rights are kept to a very small platform team.

"How would you correctly offboard a user who has run production jobs?"

Deactivate the account and strip Team/RBAC assignments rather than deleting it, so the historical audit trail of jobs they ran stays intact.

11. Wrap-Up

Key Learnings

  • A User in AAP is an identity anchor — permissions come from RBAC, not from the User object itself.
  • Normal Users can never self-escalate to admin.
  • Deactivate, don't delete, departed users with job history.
