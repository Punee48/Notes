PROJECT 05 OF 40 · INTERMEDIATE

Enterprise IAM Architecture — Guardrails, Boundaries & Break-Glass

Networking and logging are solid. Now we lock down identity itself: organization-wide guardrails no engineer can override, a permission boundary that caps what any role can ever be granted, and a tightly controlled emergency "break-glass" role for the 2am incident nobody planned for.

01 The Banking Story

Last week, a well-meaning engineer in the UAT account created an IAM role for a new Lambda function and, to "get it working quickly," attached AdministratorAccess to it, intending to scope it down later. They forgot. Three weeks later, that Lambda role still has full admin rights in UAT — an oversight, not malice, but exactly the kind of gap a real security incident would exploit.

Separately, the on-call rotation had a genuine emergency last month: Production's primary CI/CD pipeline role lost permissions after an unrelated policy cleanup, and nobody had a pre-approved way to get emergency access without waiting for the regular change process — a customer-impacting outage stretched on for 40 extra minutes waiting for approvals.

Our manager's brief: "Two things. First: make it impossible — not just discouraged — for any engineer or automated role, in any account, to ever attach full admin permissions or disable CloudTrail. Second: give on-call engineers one clearly audited break-glass role for genuine emergencies, with MFA enforced and every session logged."

02 Business Requirement, Risk & Impact

Business Requirement

No IAM principal in any account should ever be able to obtain unrestricted admin access or disable core security controls, regardless of individual mistakes.

Customer Requirement

On-call engineers need a fast, pre-approved path to emergency access during genuine incidents — without bypassing audit logging.

Current Infrastructure

Individual IAM roles created per-need, with no organization-wide ceiling on what permissions any role can ever be granted.

Existing Manual Process

Engineers self-review their own IAM policies before requesting a peer review — a process that depends entirely on individual diligence, with no automatic enforcement.

Problems With Current Process

  • A single rushed decision (like the Lambda role above) can grant unintended admin access with nothing to stop it
  • No emergency access path exists, so genuine incidents wait on the normal change process
  • No org-wide, unbypassable restriction on disabling CloudTrail, deleting the logging bucket, or leaving the Organization

Business Challenges

Regulators expect "least privilege by design," not "least privilege by policy that any engineer could technically override" — the difference matters in an audit finding.

Risk Analysis

High: an over-permissioned role in any single account is a direct path to lateral movement across the whole environment if that role's credentials are ever compromised.

Business Impact

The 40 extra minutes of unplanned outage directly translate to a customer-facing SLA breach and a mandatory incident report to the bank's risk committee.

03 Why SCPs and Permission Boundaries Together, and What We Rejected

Why we need both an SCP AND a permission boundary

A Service Control Policy (SCP) is an organization-wide ceiling — it applies to every principal in an account, including the account's own admins, and cannot be overridden from inside that account. A permission boundary is narrower — it caps what a specific role can be granted, even by someone with IAM-editing rights in that account. We need the SCP to stop "disable CloudTrail" org-wide, and the boundary to stop "grant this one Lambda role admin access" at the individual-role level — they solve different halves of the same problem.

Alternatives considered, and why they were rejected

AlternativeWhy consideredWhy rejected for ABC Bank
Rely on IAM policy review / peer review aloneNo new tooling, just process disciplineThis is exactly what failed last week — process discipline doesn't scale and doesn't prevent a rushed mistake
Permission boundaries only, no SCPsSimpler, single mechanismBoundaries only apply to roles they're explicitly attached to — they don't stop someone from creating a brand-new role without one, which an org-wide SCP does prevent structurally

04 Terraform Concepts You Must Understand Before We Build

aws_iam_policy_document Data Source

What it is: a data source that builds a valid IAM policy JSON document from native HCL blocks (statement, actions, resources, condition) instead of hand-writing or templating raw JSON.

Why it exists: HCL block syntax gets Terraform's own validation and autocomplete, and avoids JSON-escaping mistakes that are easy to make in a raw string — especially valuable for SCPs and boundaries, where a syntax mistake could silently fail to apply the intended restriction.

Production example: our SCP that denies disabling CloudTrail is built with data "aws_iam_policy_document" "deny_cloudtrail_disable", using an explicit Deny effect and a condition that still allows the Logging account itself to manage its own trail.

Alternative approach: templatefile() from Project 04 — better when a policy needs heavy looping over dynamic lists; aws_iam_policy_document is usually clearer for a fixed, well-known set of statements like an SCP.

Lifecycle Rules — prevent_destroy and ignore_changes

What it is: the lifecycle meta-argument changes how Terraform handles a specific resource during plan/apply — prevent_destroy = true makes Terraform refuse to destroy that resource even if a plan would otherwise do so; ignore_changes tells Terraform to stop reporting drift on specific attributes.

Why it exists here: the break-glass IAM role must never be accidentally destroyed by a careless terraform destroy or a refactor that renames it — prevent_destroy turns that mistake into a hard Terraform error instead of a silent, dangerous deletion.

Production example: the break-glass role resource has lifecycle { prevent_destroy = true }; removing it from the Terraform config or attempting to destroy it fails with an explicit error until an engineer deliberately removes the lifecycle block first — a deliberate two-step action, not an accident.

Common mistake: applying prevent_destroy everywhere "just to be safe" — overusing it makes legitimate refactors (like renaming a resource, which Terraform sees as destroy+create) painful; reserve it for genuinely irreplaceable or high-blast-radius resources.

Conditional Expressions for Environment-Aware Guardrails

What it is: we reuse the condition ? true_val : false_val pattern from Project 02, here to decide whether an account's SCP attachment includes the strictest "deny all except break-glass" statement, based on whether the account is tagged production.

Production example: policy_id = var.environment == "production" ? aws_organizations_policy.prod_scp.id : aws_organizations_policy.standard_scp.id — Production gets an additional layer of restriction that Dev/UAT do not.

Multiple Provider Aliases for Cross-Account SCP Attachment

What it is: SCPs are created and attached from the AWS Organizations management account, while permission boundaries and the break-glass role are created inside each individual account — this project's root module uses an aliased provider for the management-account-only Organizations resources.

05 AWS Concepts: SCPs, Permission Boundaries, and STS

What is a Service Control Policy

An organization-wide policy attached to an AWS Organizations OU or account that sets the maximum available permissions — even an account's root user cannot exceed what the SCP allows.

Why it exists

SCPs enforce guardrails structurally, at the Organizations layer, so no amount of in-account IAM misconfiguration can violate them — exactly the "disable CloudTrail" and "leave the Organization" restrictions ABC Bank needs.

What is a Permission Boundary

A managed policy attached to a specific IAM role or user that sets the maximum permissions that identity can ever be granted — even if its attached policies say otherwise, the boundary's intersection with those policies is what's actually allowed.

Why it exists

It stops the exact incident from our story: even if someone attaches AdministratorAccess to the Lambda role, the boundary intersects with it, so the role's effective permissions never exceed what the boundary allows.

What is AWS STS AssumeRole with MFA condition

The break-glass role's trust policy requires the calling principal to have authenticated with MFA within the last few minutes (aws:MultiFactorAuthAge), and its session duration is capped short (e.g. one hour).

Security

Every break-glass assumption is itself a CloudTrail event with a distinct role ARN — trivially searchable later, satisfying "who used emergency access, and when."

Limitations

SCPs are a denylist/allowlist on top of IAM, not a replacement for it — a service still needs an actual IAM policy granting an action; the SCP can only take permissions away, never grant them.

Best Practice

Test new SCPs in a dedicated "sandbox" OU first — an overly broad Deny statement can lock out an entire account, including the ability to fix the SCP itself, if applied directly to production without testing.

07 Complete Solution Design

Two SCPs are created and attached from the Organizations management account: a baseline SCP (applied to every OU) that denies disabling CloudTrail, deleting the centralized log buckets from Project 04, and leaving the Organization; and a production-only SCP (applied only to the Prod account) that additionally denies any IAM action outside of a pre-approved set, forcing all Production IAM changes through Terraform rather than console click-ops.

Inside every account, a reusable permission boundary module creates a managed policy capping any role it's attached to at a safe subset of actions (no iam:*, no organizations:*, no full *:*) — every future project's IAM roles in this series will attach this boundary by convention.

Finally, a break-glass module, deployed per account, creates one emergency-access IAM role with a strict MFA-age trust condition, a one-hour maximum session, AdministratorAccess attached (deliberately powerful, for genuine emergencies only), and an EventBridge rule + SNS topic that immediately alerts the security team the moment it's assumed.

environments/management/main.tf — Baseline SCP

environments/management/main.tf
data "aws_iam_policy_document" "baseline_scp" {                  # Builds the SCP's JSON from readable HCL blocks instead of raw JSON
  statement {
    sid    = "DenyDisableCloudTrail"
    effect = "Deny"                                               # SCPs are almost always Deny statements — they remove permissions, never grant them
    actions = [
      "cloudtrail:StopLogging",
      "cloudtrail:DeleteTrail",
      "cloudtrail:UpdateTrail",
    ]
    resources = ["*"]
  }

  statement {
    sid    = "DenyLeaveOrganization"
    effect = "Deny"
    actions   = ["organizations:LeaveOrganization"]
    resources = ["*"]
  }

  statement {
    sid    = "DenyDeleteCentralLogBuckets"
    effect = "Deny"
    actions = [
      "s3:DeleteBucket",
      "s3:PutBucketPolicy",                                        # Also blocks silently rewriting the bucket policy to something permissive
      "s3:PutLifecycleConfiguration",
    ]
    resources = [
      "arn:aws:s3:::abc-bank-central-flow-logs-2026",
      "arn:aws:s3:::abc-bank-central-flow-logs-2026/*",
    ]

    condition {
      test     = "StringNotEquals"                                  # Exception: the Logging account itself is still allowed to manage its own bucket
      variable = "aws:PrincipalAccount"
      values   = [var.logging_account_id]
    }
  }
}

resource "aws_organizations_policy" "baseline_scp" {
  name    = "abc-bank-baseline-guardrails"
  type    = "SERVICE_CONTROL_POLICY"
  content = data.aws_iam_policy_document.baseline_scp.json           # Directly uses the rendered JSON from the data source above
}

modules/permission-boundary/main.tf

modules/permission-boundary/main.tf
data "aws_iam_policy_document" "boundary" {
  statement {
    sid       = "AllowMostServices"
    effect    = "Allow"
    actions   = ["s3:*", "dynamodb:*", "lambda:*", "logs:*", "sqs:*", "sns:*"]  # Deliberately broad ALLOW — the DENY statements below are what actually enforce the boundary
    resources = ["*"]
  }

  statement {
    sid       = "DenyIAMAndOrgActions"                                # No role bounded by this policy can ever create/modify IAM or Organizations resources
    effect    = "Deny"
    actions   = ["iam:*", "organizations:*", "sts:AssumeRole"]
    resources = ["*"]
  }

  statement {
    sid       = "DenyFullAdmin"
    effect    = "Deny"
    actions   = ["*"]
    resources = ["*"]
    condition {
      test     = "StringEquals"
      variable = "iam:PolicyARN"                                      # Explicitly blocks the AdministratorAccess managed policy from ever being effective, even if attached
      values   = ["arn:aws:iam::aws:policy/AdministratorAccess"]
    }
  }
}

resource "aws_iam_policy" "boundary" {
  name   = "abc-bank-standard-permission-boundary"
  policy = data.aws_iam_policy_document.boundary.json
}

modules/break-glass-role/main.tf

modules/break-glass-role/main.tf
data "aws_iam_policy_document" "trust" {
  statement {
    effect  = "Allow"
    actions = ["sts:AssumeRole"]
    principals {
      type        = "AWS"
      identifiers = [var.trusted_group_arn]
    }
    condition {
      test     = "NumericLessThan"
      variable = "aws:MultiFactorAuthAge"                             # Requires MFA to have happened within the last 5 minutes (300 seconds) — not just "at some point"
      values   = ["300"]
    }
    condition {
      test     = "Bool"
      variable = "aws:MultiFactorAuthPresent"                          # Belt-and-braces: also requires MFA to be present on the session at all
      values   = ["true"]
    }
  }
}

resource "aws_iam_role" "break_glass" {
  name                 = "break-glass-emergency-access"
  assume_role_policy   = data.aws_iam_policy_document.trust.json
  max_session_duration = 3600                                          # Hard cap of 1 hour — forces re-authentication for any incident lasting longer

  lifecycle {
    prevent_destroy = true                                              # This role must never be accidentally destroyed by a refactor or careless destroy
  }

  tags = { Purpose = "emergency-access", RequiresSecurityReview = "true" }
}

resource "aws_iam_role_policy_attachment" "admin" {                     # Deliberately powerful — this IS the point of a break-glass role
  role       = aws_iam_role.break_glass.name
  policy_arn = "arn:aws:iam::aws:policy/AdministratorAccess"
}

resource "aws_cloudwatch_event_rule" "break_glass_used" {                # Watches CloudTrail for any AssumeRole event against this specific role
  name = "break-glass-role-assumed"
  event_pattern = jsonencode({                                           # jsonencode() here — a simple fixed structure, no looping needed, so no templatefile required
    source      = ["aws.sts"]
    detail-type = ["AWS API Call via CloudTrail"]
    detail = {
      eventName  = ["AssumeRole"]
      requestParameters = { roleArn = [aws_iam_role.break_glass.arn] }
    }
  })
}

resource "aws_sns_topic" "break_glass_alert" {
  name = "break-glass-alerts"
}

resource "aws_sns_topic_subscription" "security_team" {
  topic_arn = aws_sns_topic.break_glass_alert.arn
  protocol  = "email"
  endpoint  = var.alert_email
}

resource "aws_cloudwatch_event_target" "notify" {
  rule      = aws_cloudwatch_event_rule.break_glass_used.name
  arn       = aws_sns_topic.break_glass_alert.arn
}

SCP DenyFullAdmin statement's condition on iam:PolicyARNDenies any action when the specific AdministratorAccess managed policy is the one being evaluatedAssuming a Deny on iam:AttachRolePolicy alone would be enough — that would stop attaching it going forward, but wouldn't neutralize policies already attached before the SCP existed; the boundary's runtime Deny is what covers both cases

11 Execution Flow, Explained Internally

terraform plan (management account)

Shows the SCP's rendered JSON from the aws_iam_policy_document data source as part of the plan — always read this JSON carefully in the PR review; an overly broad Deny here can lock out an entire OU.

terraform apply (permission boundary)

Creates the boundary policy as a standalone resource with no dependents yet — it only takes effect on a role once that role's own resource sets permissions_boundary to point at it.

terraform destroy (break-glass module)

Fails immediately on the aws_iam_role.break_glass resource due to prevent_destroy — Terraform prints an explicit error rather than silently skipping or force-destroying it.

Always test a new or modified SCP against a sandbox OU first. An SCP is enforced immediately upon attachment — if it accidentally denies the Organizations or IAM actions needed to fix itself, recovery requires management-account-level intervention outside of Terraform.

12 CI/CD Integration

.github/workflows/terraform-iam-guardrails.yml
name: terraform-iam-guardrails
on:
  pull_request:
    paths: ["environments/management/**", "modules/permission-boundary/**", "modules/break-glass-role/**"]

jobs:
  plan-and-review:
    runs-on: ubuntu-latest
    defaults: { run: { working-directory: environments/management } }
    steps:
      - uses: actions/checkout@v4
      - uses: hashicorp/setup-terraform@v3
      - run: terraform init
      - run: terraform validate
      - name: Plan
        run: terraform plan -no-color | tee plan-output.txt
      - name: Post plan as PR comment
        uses: actions/github-script@v7                                  # Posts the SCP's rendered JSON as a PR comment so reviewers see the EXACT policy before merge
        with:
          script: |
            const fs = require('fs');
            const plan = fs.readFileSync('plan-output.txt', 'utf8');
            github.rest.issues.createComment({
              issue_number: context.issue.number,
              owner: context.repo.owner,
              repo: context.repo.repo,
              body: "```\n" + plan.slice(0, 60000) + "\n```"
            });
Never auto-apply changes to SCPs or permission boundaries from CI/CD — require a second human reviewer (in addition to the PR author) specifically for any file under environments/management/ or the permission boundary module.

14 Interview Preparation

Q: What's the practical difference between an SCP and a permission boundary?
An SCP is an org-wide ceiling enforced by AWS Organizations across every principal in an account, including its root user; a permission boundary is attached to one specific IAM role or user and caps only that identity — you typically need both for full-depth defense.
Q: If a role has AdministratorAccess attached AND a permission boundary, what happens?
The role's effective permissions are the intersection of the attached policy and the boundary — AdministratorAccess grants everything, but the boundary's Deny statements still remove whatever it explicitly denies, regardless of what's attached.
Q: Why require aws:MultiFactorAuthAge instead of just aws:MultiFactorAuthPresent for break-glass access?
MultiFactorAuthPresent only proves MFA happened at some point in the session's lifetime; MultiFactorAuthAge lets you require it happened recently, preventing a long-lived session token from being used for emergency access long after the original MFA check.
Q: Why use prevent_destroy on the break-glass role but not on every resource?
It's reserved for genuinely high-blast-radius or hard-to-recreate resources; applying it broadly makes routine refactors (which Terraform often implements as destroy+recreate) unnecessarily painful across the whole codebase.
Manager round: How would you safely roll out a new, stricter SCP across the whole Organization?
Attach it to a sandbox OU first, verify expected behavior with real test accounts, then roll it out to lower-risk OUs (Dev/UAT) before Production, watching for unexpected AccessDenied errors at each stage.
