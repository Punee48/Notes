PROJECT 10 OF 40 · INTERMEDIATE→ADVANCED

Multi-Region Disaster Recovery — Active-Passive Across Two Regions

Everything we've built so far lives entirely in ap-south-1. A regional AWS outage — rare, but not theoretical — would take the whole Digital Banking Platform down with it. Today we stand up a passive twin in a second region, with data replicated continuously and DNS ready to fail over the moment it's needed.

01 The Banking Story

The bank's risk committee has flagged a gap that's been quietly deferred since the program started: every piece of infrastructure we've built across all nine prior projects lives in a single AWS region, ap-south-1. AWS regions are highly reliable, but they are not immune to multi-hour outages — and for a bank, "our core banking platform is unreachable" for even 30 minutes is a regulatory reportable event, not just an inconvenience.

The bank has committed to a Recovery Time Objective (RTO) of 15 minutes and a Recovery Point Objective (RPO) of 5 minutes for the payments-api service specifically — meaning: if ap-south-1 goes down, payments must be reachable again within 15 minutes, having lost no more than 5 minutes of data.

Our manager's brief: "Stand up a passive DR environment in ap-southeast-1. Replicate the database continuously. Replicate what needs replicating in S3. And wire up Route53 so that when — not if, eventually — we need to fail over, it's a deliberate, tested, fast action, not a multi-day scramble."

02 Business Requirement, Risk & Impact

Business Requirement

The payments-api service must meet a 15-minute RTO and 5-minute RPO in the event of a full regional outage in ap-south-1.

Customer Requirement

The bank's risk and compliance function needs a documented, periodically tested DR plan — not just infrastructure that theoretically could fail over.

Current Infrastructure

The complete VPC framework, Transit Gateway, logging, IAM, secrets, and DNS layers from Projects 02-09, entirely single-region.

Existing Manual Process

None — there is currently no DR capability at all for this platform; a regional outage today would be a full, unplanned outage with no defined recovery path.

Problems With Current Process

  • Zero resilience to a regional-scale AWS event
  • No tested failover procedure means even a manual, ad hoc recovery would likely take far longer than 15 minutes
  • No visibility into how much data would actually be lost in a real event, since replication doesn't yet exist

Business Challenges

Banking regulators in most jurisdictions require demonstrable DR capability with a defined and tested RTO/RPO for core payment systems specifically — this is often a licensing condition, not an optional best practice.

Risk Analysis

High: a regional outage without DR is a low-probability but severe-impact event — exactly the risk profile that demands a mitigating control, even though it may never be invoked.

Business Impact

Failure to demonstrate DR capability during a regulatory exam can result in formal findings and, in serious cases, restrictions on the bank's ability to process payments until remediated.

03 Why Active-Passive, and What We Rejected

Why Active-Passive DR (not Active-Active)

An active-passive design keeps the DR region's compute scaled down (or fully stopped) most of the time, with data replication running continuously — meeting the RTO/RPO targets at a fraction of the cost of running full production capacity in two regions simultaneously. Active-active would meet an even tighter RTO, but ABC Bank's committed 15-minute RTO doesn't require it, and the operational complexity and cost of keeping two fully active regions in sync isn't justified by the actual requirement.

Alternatives considered, and why they were rejected

AlternativeWhy consideredWhy rejected for ABC Bank
Active-Active, both regions serving trafficNear-zero RTO, no failover decision neededSignificantly higher cost and operational complexity (bi-directional replication conflicts, dual-write consistency) than the bank's actual 15-minute RTO requirement justifies
Backup-and-restore only (no live replication)Cheapest option, simplest to buildCannot meet a 5-minute RPO or 15-minute RTO — restoring from a backup at this scale takes hours, not minutes
AWS Elastic Disaster Recovery (block-level replication)Purpose-built DR service, application-agnosticBetter suited to lift-and-shift/on-prem DR (a later project in this series) — for our own Terraform-managed cloud-native stack, native per-service replication (RDS, S3) gives us more precise control over what replicates and how

04 Terraform Concepts You Must Understand Before We Build

Multiple Provider Configurations for Multi-Region

What it is: two full provider "aws" blocks in the same configuration — one default for ap-south-1, one aliased for ap-southeast-1 — letting a single Terraform apply manage resources in both regions from one root module.

Why it exists: the DR VPC, its Transit Gateway peering attachment, and cross-region replication configuration all need to exist in relation to BOTH regions simultaneously — modeling that with two separate root modules would make the relationship between them (which this project's whole purpose depends on) much harder to express and review.

Production example: our vpc-framework module (from Project 02) is called twice in this same file — once with the default provider, once with providers = { aws = aws.dr_region } — reusing the exact same versioned module for both regions.

Reinforcing Nested Modules Across Regions

What it is: the same terraform-aws-vpc-framework module from Project 02, called a second time with different CIDR and region inputs — proof that a properly versioned, reusable module pays off exactly when a need like this arises months later.

Production example: module "vpc_dr" { source = "git::...?ref=v1.2.0" providers = { aws = aws.dr_region } vpc_cidr = "10.20.0.0/16" ... } — same tested module, same version, completely different region.

Conditional Resource Creation for Active-Passive Scaling

What it is: using count = var.dr_active ? 1 : 0 on the DR region's compute resources, so the same Terraform code can represent both the normal "passive, scaled to zero" state and, during an actual failover drill or real event, the "active" state — flipped by a single variable, not a code rewrite.

Why it exists: keeping the passive region's compute defined in code (just not created) means the failover procedure is "change one variable and apply," not "write new Terraform under pressure during an actual incident."

05 AWS Concepts: Cross-Region Replication & Failover

RDS Cross-Region Read Replica

A continuously replicating, read-only copy of the Production database in the DR region — promotable to a standalone read-write instance in minutes during an actual failover, rather than restored from a backup.

S3 Cross-Region Replication (CRR)

Automatically and asynchronously copies new objects from the primary region's buckets (e.g. application assets, the centralized logs from Project 04) to a matching bucket in the DR region, typically within minutes of the original write.

Route53 Health Checks + Failover Routing

A health check continuously probes the primary region's endpoint; a failover routing policy automatically shifts DNS answers to the DR region's endpoint the moment the primary is detected as unhealthy — the actual mechanism that makes the 15-minute RTO achievable without a human manually editing DNS at 3am.

Why Continuous Replication, Not Periodic Backups

A 5-minute RPO is incompatible with periodic (e.g. nightly) backups — only continuous, near-real-time replication (RDS read replicas, S3 CRR) can keep data loss that low.

Security

The DR region needs its own complete security stack — IAM guardrails from Project 05, VPC endpoints from Project 08 — DR is not exempt from the same controls Production requires; a insecure DR region is itself a risk.

Cost

Active-passive keeps DR compute costs low (often just the replica/replication costs) until an actual failover, at which point compute is scaled up — a deliberate, budgeted cost trade-off against the alternative of full active-active spend.

Limitations

A promoted read replica becomes a new, independent database — application connection strings must point at the new endpoint, which is exactly what the Route53 failover record's automatic switch is designed to handle transparently.

07 Complete Solution Design

A second full instance of the Project 02 vpc-framework module is deployed into ap-southeast-1 using an aliased provider, with compute resources gated behind count = var.dr_active ? 1 : 0 (defaulting to false — passive). An RDS read replica in the DR region continuously replicates from Production's primary instance; S3 Cross-Region Replication rules mirror the primary buckets from Projects 04 and 06's related infrastructure into matching DR-region buckets.

A Route53 health check monitors the primary region's payments-api endpoint; a failover routing policy pair (PRIMARY/SECONDARY records under the same name) automatically returns the DR region's endpoint the moment the health check fails — the DNS-level mechanism that actually delivers the RTO, once an engineer has promoted the replica and scaled up DR compute.

environments/dr/providers.tf

environments/dr/providers.tf
provider "aws" {                                                     # Default provider — the PRIMARY region, unaliased
  region = "ap-south-1"
}

provider "aws" {
  alias  = "dr_region"                                                 # Aliased provider — every DR-region resource explicitly references this
  region = "ap-southeast-1"
}

environments/dr/main.tf

environments/dr/main.tf
module "vpc_dr" {                                                     # Same versioned module from Project 02 — proof of what proper versioning buys you later
  source = "git::https://github.com/abc-bank/terraform-aws-vpc-framework.git?ref=v1.2.0"
  providers = {
    aws = aws.dr_region                                                 # Tells the module to use the DR-region provider for every resource it creates
  }

  vpc_name = "abc-bank-dr-vpc"
  vpc_cidr = "10.20.0.0/16"                                             # Deliberately non-overlapping with Prod's 10.10.0.0/16 — required for the eventual TGW peering
  ha_nat   = false                                                       # Passive region doesn't need HA NAT until an actual failover promotes it to active

  subnet_definitions = [
    { name = "public-a",  cidr_block = "10.20.0.0/24",  availability_zone = "ap-southeast-1a", tier = "public" },
    { name = "private-a", cidr_block = "10.20.10.0/24", availability_zone = "ap-southeast-1a", tier = "private" },
    { name = "db-a",      cidr_block = "10.20.20.0/24", availability_zone = "ap-southeast-1a", tier = "database" },
  ]

  tags = { Environment = "dr" }
}

resource "aws_instance" "dr_app" {                                     # Deliberately created only when DR is actively invoked
  count = var.dr_active ? 1 : 0                                         # Conditional resource creation — the passive/active switch for this entire fleet

  ami           = data.aws_ssm_parameter.latest_al2023_ami_dr.value
  instance_type = "t3.large"
  subnet_id     = module.vpc_dr.subnet_ids_by_tier["private"][0]

  tags = { Name = "payments-api-app-dr" }
}

environments/dr/replication.tf

environments/dr/replication.tf
resource "aws_db_instance" "payments_replica" {
  provider = aws.dr_region                                              # Created in the DR region, but references the PRIMARY region's database as its source

  replicate_source_db = var.primary_db_arn                               # The ARN of the Prod RDS instance from earlier in this series — enables continuous cross-region replication
  instance_class       = "db.r6g.large"
  db_subnet_group_name = module.vpc_dr.database_subnet_group_name

  tags = { Name = "payments-api-db-dr-replica", Role = "disaster-recovery" }
}

resource "aws_s3_bucket_replication_configuration" "logs_crr" {
  provider = aws.dr_region_unused_intentionally_left_default            # NOTE: replication config lives on the SOURCE bucket's own provider (primary region), not the destination
  role     = aws_iam_role.s3_replication.arn
  bucket   = var.primary_logging_bucket_id                                # The centralized logging bucket from Project 04

  rule {
    id     = "replicate-to-dr"
    status = "Enabled"

    destination {
      bucket        = "arn:aws:s3:::abc-bank-central-flow-logs-dr-2026"    # A matching bucket created in the DR region, referenced by ARN
      storage_class = "STANDARD_IA"                                         # Infrequent Access — the DR copy is rarely read except during an actual failover or audit
    }
  }
}

environments/dr/failover-dns.tf

environments/dr/failover-dns.tf
resource "aws_route53_health_check" "primary" {
  fqdn              = var.primary_alb_dns_name
  port              = 443
  type              = "HTTPS"
  resource_path     = "/healthz"                                        # Application-level health endpoint — a real functional check, not just "is the ALB reachable"
  failure_threshold = 3                                                  # Requires 3 consecutive failures before marking unhealthy — avoids a single transient blip triggering failover# Same name for BOTH records — clients never need to know which region answered
  type            = "A"
  set_identifier  = "primary"
  health_check_id = aws_route53_health_check.primary.id

  failover_routing_policy {
    type = "PRIMARY"
  }

  alias {
    name                   = var.primary_alb_dns_name
    zone_id                = var.primary_alb_zone_id
    evaluate_target_health = true
  }
}

resource "aws_route53_record" "payments_api_secondary" {
  zone_id        = var.hosted_zone_id
  name           = "payments-api.abcbank.internal"
  type           = "A"
  set_identifier = "secondary"

  failover_routing_policy {
    type = "SECONDARY"                                                    # Only answered by Route53 when the PRIMARY record's health check is failing
  }

  alias {
    name                   = var.dr_alb_dns_name
    zone_id                = var.dr_alb_zone_id
    evaluate_target_health = false                                         # DR's own ALB may have nothing behind it while passive — don't health-check what isn't running yet
  }
}

10 Code Explanation Summary

Block / ArgumentWhat it doesCommon mistake to avoid
providers = { aws = aws.dr_region } on a module callTells a child module which specific provider configuration to use, overriding its own defaultForgetting this on a module call — the module would silently use the DEFAULT (primary region) provider, creating DR resources in the wrong region entirely
count = var.dr_active ? 1 : 0Conditionally creates (or removes) a resource based on a boolean variable, without deleting the resource block from the codeDeleting and re-adding the resource block by hand during an actual incident — keeping it permanently in code with this conditional is what makes failover a one-variable change, not an emergency code-write
replicate_source_dbTurns an aws_db_instance into a continuously-replicating read replica of another instance, potentially cross-regionConfusing this with a manual snapshot-restore approach — replication here is continuous and ongoing, not a one-time copy
Two records with the same name but different failover_routing_policyRoute53's mechanism for automatic failover — both records exist simultaneously, and health check status decides which one answersSetting evaluate_target_health = true on the SECONDARY record before DR is actively serving traffic — Route53 could then consider the failover target itself unhealthy and have nowhere left to send traffic

11 Execution Flow, Explained Internally

terraform apply — steady state (dr_active = false)

Creates the DR VPC, the RDS read replica (continuously replicating), and the S3 replication configuration — but zero DR compute instances, since count evaluates to 0.

terraform apply — during an actual failover (dr_active = true)

The plan shows exactly the DR compute instances being added — nothing about the already-existing VPC, replica, or DNS records changes, since only the count-gated resources were affected by the variable flip.

Route53 during an outage

Operates entirely independently of Terraform — the health check and failover routing react to the primary's actual availability in real time, seconds after 3 consecutive failed probes, with no Terraform apply needed for the DNS switch itself.

Promoting the RDS read replica to a standalone writable instance is a one-way action — once promoted, it stops replicating from the original primary. Only do this during a genuine failover or a carefully scoped DR drill, never as a routine test against the real replica.

12 CI/CD Integration

.github/workflows/terraform-dr-failover.yml
name: terraform-dr-failover
on:
  workflow_dispatch:                                                     # Manually triggered ONLY — never on a routine push, given the severity of what this pipeline can do
    inputs:
      dr_active:
        description: "Set to true to activate DR compute (real failover or approved drill)"
        required: true
        default: "false"

jobs:
  failover:
    runs-on: ubuntu-latest
    defaults: { run: { working-directory: environments/dr } }
    steps:
      - uses: actions/checkout@v4
      - uses: hashicorp/setup-terraform@v3
      - run: terraform init
      - run: terraform plan  -var="dr_active=${{ github.event.inputs.dr_active }}" -out=tfplan
      - name: Require manual approval before DR activation
        uses: trstringer/manual-approval@v1
        with:
          approvers: incident-commander,platform-lead
      - run: terraform apply -auto-approve tfplan
workflow_dispatch with a mandatory approval gate ensures DR activation is always a deliberate human decision — appropriate for an action with this much operational and cost impact, never something that fires automatically off a code push.

14 Interview Preparation

Q: What's the difference between RTO and RPO, and how does this design meet both?
RTO is how long recovery takes; RPO is how much data loss is acceptable — continuous RDS/S3 replication keeps RPO low (data is nearly current in the DR region at all times), while pre-provisioned (but scaled-to-zero) infrastructure plus automated Route53 failover keeps RTO low by removing manual setup from the critical path.
Q: Why gate DR compute behind a count-based conditional instead of just not writing that Terraform until it's needed?
Having the resource fully defined in code, just not created, means activation is a single variable change and a normal apply — writing new Terraform under the pressure of an actual incident is slower and far more error-prone.
Q: Why does the SECONDARY Route53 record set evaluate_target_health to false?
Because the DR region's own ALB may have nothing running behind it while passive — health-checking a target that isn't meant to be serving traffic yet could leave Route53 with no healthy answer for either record during normal operation.
Q: Why is promoting a read replica a one-way action, and what does that mean operationally?
Promotion breaks replication from the source permanently, turning the replica into an independent writable database — it should only be done during a genuine failover or a carefully scoped, approved drill, never as a routine or accidental test.
Manager round: How would you prove to a regulator that your DR plan actually works, not just exists on paper?
Show a log of actual, periodically executed DR drills with measured RTO figures against the committed target, plus the runbook and approval process used each time — demonstrated, repeatable evidence, not just the presence of Terraform code.
