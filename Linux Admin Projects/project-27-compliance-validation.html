Compliance Validation

Weeks of manual evidence-gathering, and an auditor rejected half of it anyway — because nobody could prove the evidence hadn't been edited after the fact.

🎯 Level: Advanced 🧠 New Concepts: 8 🏗️ Environment: Linux / WSL ⏱️ Duration: 3 hrs

📖 The Story — Evidence Nobody Could Prove Was Real

NovaTrust's regulatory compliance audit required the compliance team to demonstrate, control by control, that specific banking security requirements were actually being met — access control, monitoring, patch management, and dozens more. The team spent weeks manually cross-referencing outputs from a dozen different monitoring systems, screenshotting logs, and assembling documents by hand. The auditor rejected several submissions outright: nothing proved the evidence hadn't simply been edited or fabricated after the fact — no timestamp integrity, no way to verify the screenshot matched what a system had actually reported.

Priya's assignment: "We have 26 projects' worth of real technical evidence already being generated every single day — we just never mapped any of it to what regulators actually ask for, or made it tamper-evident. I want a script that maps our existing checks to specific named compliance controls, generates one evidence package with a cryptographic integrity proof, and produces something we can hand an auditor directly, with confidence nobody can credibly claim it was altered."

This final "meta" project doesn't introduce new monitoring — it maps and packages the results of everything built across this entire course into a form regulators specifically need.
🔁 Reinforced from Projects 1–26
Project 11's sha256sum integrity verification, Project 25's report-aggregation philosophy, and Project 5/23's retention-policy thinking all converge here, now protecting compliance evidence specifically.

🏛️ Business Requirement & Banking Scenario

Business Requirement

Map existing technical monitoring evidence to specific regulatory compliance controls and produce a tamper-evident, audit-ready evidence package.

Customer Requirement

Compliance and Audit need evidence they can hand directly to a regulator with cryptographic proof of when it was generated and that it hasn't been altered since.

Banking Scenario

NovaTrust operates under banking regulatory requirements mandating demonstrable, control-by-control evidence of specific security and operational practices, verified during periodic regulatory audits.

Existing Manual Process / Prior Automation Gap

Compliance evidence was assembled entirely by hand, weeks of cross-referencing 26 separate systems' outputs with no systematic mapping to actual regulatory control requirements and no integrity proof on the final assembled evidence.

Problems With Manual Evidence Assembly

Slow, error-prone, inconsistent between audit cycles, and critically, unable to prove authenticity — an auditor has no way to verify a manually-compiled document wasn't edited after the systems it describes actually ran.

Business Impact & Risk

Rejected evidence submissions delay regulatory sign-off and raise serious questions about the organization's overall compliance posture and evidentiary discipline, independent of whether the underlying controls were actually being met.

Why Automation Is Required

Only an automated process can consistently map technical evidence to regulatory controls and produce genuinely tamper-evident output, at a speed and reliability no manual process could match every audit cycle.

Solution Design

A controls-mapping registry links each named regulatory control to one or more existing monitoring projects' evidence sources; the script aggregates each control's evidence, classifies it SATISFIED/NOT_SATISFIED/NOT_ASSESSED, generates a checksummed evidence manifest, and packages everything with a final integrity proof.

Project Scope

In scope: control-to-evidence mapping, aggregation, tamper-evident packaging with checksums, and a control-by-control report. Out of scope: determining which specific regulatory framework's exact control language to use — that mapping decision belongs to Compliance/Legal, not to this script's own logic.

Expected Outcome

One command producing a complete, cryptographically verifiable compliance evidence package — replacing weeks of manual, unverifiable assembly with minutes of automated, tamper-evident reporting.

🧠 New Bash Concepts for This Project

1. A Controls-Mapping Registry (New Registry Type)

What: A config file linking a named control ID (e.g., AC-3) to a human description and one or more evidence-source references — pointing at specific prior projects' log/report locations.
Why: This is the crucial translation layer between "what our systems technically check" and "what a specific named regulatory requirement actually asks for" — a mapping decision that belongs jointly to engineering and compliance.

2. Aggregating Multiple Evidence Sources Per Control

What: Some controls require more than one technical check to be genuinely satisfied — e.g., an access-control requirement might need both Project 10's user-lifecycle evidence and Project 26's sudo-access audit evidence, both passing.
Why: Real regulatory controls are often broader than any single technical check can fully address — accurately representing this requires the mapping to support one-to-many relationships, not just one-to-one.

3. A Three-State Control Status (New Taxonomy, Again)

What: SATISFIED / NOT_SATISFIED / NOT_ASSESSED — yet another domain-specific status vocabulary (following Project 26's own distinct severity model), reflecting genuinely different meanings than pass/fail alone.
Why: NOT_ASSESSED (no technical evidence source mapped yet) is a fundamentally different, and importantly less alarming, finding than NOT_SATISFIED (evidence exists and shows the control isn't being met) — conflating them would either create false alarm or false confidence.

4. Individually Checksumming Every Piece of Evidence (Reinforced Project 11)

What: Generating a sha256sum for every individual log/report file referenced as evidence, exactly as Project 11 verified backup archives — now applied to compliance evidence specifically.
Why: Each individual piece of evidence needs its own verifiable fingerprint, so any single file's integrity can be checked independently.

5. A "Manifest of Manifests" (Checksum of Checksums)

What: After generating individual checksums for every evidence file, those checksums themselves are collected into one manifest file — which is then itself checksummed, creating one final "root" checksum that indirectly verifies everything beneath it.
Why: A single root checksum, if independently recorded (e.g., communicated to the auditor through a separate channel, or notarized), provides one simple, verifiable proof that the entire evidence package — every file and every individual checksum — is exactly as it was at generation time; changing even one byte anywhere in the package would change this final root checksum.

6. A Definitive Generation Timestamp

What: Recording the exact date and time the evidence package was assembled, embedded directly in the report and manifest — reinforcing the timestamping discipline present in every project's logging since Project 1, now serving a specifically evidentiary purpose.
Why: An auditor needs to know precisely when a snapshot was taken relative to the period being audited — an undated or ambiguously-dated evidence package is itself a common reason for rejection.

7. Longer-Term Evidence Retention (Reinforced Project 5, Different Timescale)

What: Compliance evidence packages typically require multi-year retention per regulatory mandate — a fundamentally different, much longer retention policy than Project 5's operational log rotation, even though the underlying find -mtime mechanics are identical.
Why: Applying Project 5's routine log-rotation retention period to regulatory evidence would be a serious compliance mistake — evidence archives need their own explicit, typically much longer, retention configuration.

8. This Project as a Capstone Aggregation Layer (Conceptual)

What: Like Project 25, this script reads and packages existing results rather than performing new technical checks itself — but one layer more abstract, mapping to regulatory language rather than just consolidating operational status.
Why: Recognizing the difference between Project 25's operational aggregation (for leadership) and this project's compliance aggregation (for regulators) illustrates how the same underlying technique — reading and synthesizing existing evidence — serves genuinely different audiences and purposes.

🧩 Utility Functions

scripts/utils.sh
#!/bin/bash
# =====================================================
# utils.sh - Compliance validation helper functions
# =====================================================

log_message() {
    local level="$1"; local message="$2"; local timestamp
    timestamp=$(date '+%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S')
    echo "[$timestamp] [$level] $message" | tee -a "$LOG_FILE"
}

# ---------- source_indicates_pass ----------
# Purpose: Return 0 if the latest relevant evidence at a source path indicates a healthy/passing result
source_indicates_pass() {
    local source_path="$1"
    [[ -e "$source_path" ]] || return 1

    local latest_evidence
    if [[ -d "$source_path" ]]; then
        latest_evidence=$(find "$source_path" -type f -newermt "$(date -d '2 days ago')" 2>/dev/null | sort | tail -n 1)
    else
        latest_evidence="$source_path"
    fi
    [[ -z "$latest_evidence" ]] && return 1

    # Absence of failure-indicating keywords in the most recent evidence is treated as passing
    ! grep -qiE "critical|fail|breach|non_compliant" "$latest_evidence" 2>/dev/null
}

# ---------- evaluate_control ----------
# Purpose: Return SATISFIED, NOT_SATISFIED, or NOT_ASSESSED for one control's comma-separated evidence sources
evaluate_control() {
    local sources_csv="$1"
    [[ -z "$sources_csv" ]] && { echo "NOT_ASSESSED"; return; }

    local IFS=','
    local sources=($sources_csv)
    for source in "${sources[@]}"; do
        if ! source_indicates_pass "$source"; then
            echo "NOT_SATISFIED"
            return
        fi
    done
    echo "SATISFIED"
}

# ---------- add_to_evidence_manifest ----------
add_to_evidence_manifest() {
    local file_path="$1"
    local manifest_file="$2"
    [[ -f "$file_path" ]] || return
    local checksum
    checksum=$(sha256sum "$file_path" | awk '{print $1}')
    echo "${checksum}  ${file_path}" >> "$manifest_file"
}

Line-by-Line Highlights

  • find "$source_path" -type f -newermt "$(date -d '2 days ago')" — -newermt (a variant of find's time-comparison options) matches files modified more recently than a given date string directly, a cleaner alternative to computing an epoch cutoff manually for this particular use case.
  • local IFS=','; local sources=($sources_csv) — temporarily changing IFS to a comma specifically for this one array-assignment line splits the comma-separated evidence-source list into a proper Bash array; scoping the IFS change to a local variable prevents it from affecting anything elsewhere in the script.
  • evaluate_control's loop returns NOT_SATISFIED immediately on the first failing source — consistent with Project 24's chained-verification short-circuit philosophy, since one failing required source is already enough to determine the control isn't satisfied.
  • add_to_evidence_manifest writes each evidence file's checksum in the exact same checksum␣␣filepath format sha256sum itself produces — meaning the resulting manifest file could even be independently re-verified later with a plain sha256sum -c, exactly as in Project 11.

🩺 Main Script

scripts/compliance_validation.sh
#!/bin/bash
# =====================================================================
# compliance_validation.sh
# NovaTrust Bank - Regulatory Compliance Evidence Aggregation & Packaging
# =====================================================================

set -uo pipefail

SCRIPT_DIR="$(cd "$(dirname "${BASH_SOURCE[0]}")" && pwd)"
source "$SCRIPT_DIR/utils.sh"
source "$SCRIPT_DIR/../config/compliance_validation.conf"

REGISTRY_FILE="$SCRIPT_DIR/../config/controls_registry.txt"
[[ -f "$REGISTRY_FILE" ]] || { echo "ERROR: Controls registry not found: $REGISTRY_FILE" >&2; exit 1; }

TODAY=$(date '+%Y%m%d')
GENERATED_AT=$(date '+%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S %Z')
LOG_FILE="$SCRIPT_DIR/$LOG_DIR/compliance_validation_${TODAY}.log"
PACKAGE_DIR="$SCRIPT_DIR/$PACKAGE_DIR/$TODAY"
mkdir -p "$(dirname "$LOG_FILE")" "$PACKAGE_DIR"

MANIFEST_FILE="$PACKAGE_DIR/evidence_manifest.txt"
REPORT_FILE="$PACKAGE_DIR/compliance_report.html"
: > "$MANIFEST_FILE"                                 # Truncate/create empty - this run's manifest starts fresh

log_message "INFO" "=== Starting compliance evidence aggregation (as of $GENERATED_AT) ==="

TABLE_ROWS=""
SATISFIED_COUNT=0
TOTAL_CONTROLS=0

while IFS=':' read -r control_id description sources_csv; do
    control_id=$(echo "$control_id" | xargs)
    description=$(echo "$description" | xargs)
    sources_csv=$(echo "$sources_csv" | xargs)
    [[ -z "$control_id" || "$control_id" == \#* ]] && continue
    ((TOTAL_CONTROLS++))

    STATUS=$(evaluate_control "$sources_csv")
    log_message "INFO" "$control_id: $STATUS - $description"

    # Add each referenced evidence file/directory's contents to the tamper-evident manifest
    if [[ -n "$sources_csv" ]]; then
        IFS=',' read -ra SOURCE_LIST <<< "$sources_csv"
        for source in "${SOURCE_LIST[@]}"; do
            if [[ -d "$source" ]]; then
                while IFS= read -r f; do add_to_evidence_manifest "$f" "$MANIFEST_FILE"; done < <(find "$source" -type f 2>/dev/null)
            elif [[ -f "$source" ]]; then
                add_to_evidence_manifest "$source" "$MANIFEST_FILE"
            fi
        done
    fi

    ROW_COLOR="#1b7f4c"
    [[ "$STATUS" == "NOT_SATISFIED" ]] && ROW_COLOR="#a3242f"
    [[ "$STATUS" == "NOT_ASSESSED" ]] && ROW_COLOR="#6b7280"
    TABLE_ROWS+="${control_id}${description}${STATUS}"

    [[ "$STATUS" == "SATISFIED" ]] && ((SATISFIED_COUNT++))

done < "$REGISTRY_FILE"

# ---------- Checksum the manifest itself: the "root" tamper-evident proof ----------
MANIFEST_CHECKSUM=$(sha256sum "$MANIFEST_FILE" | awk '{print $1}')
echo "$MANIFEST_CHECKSUM  $(basename "$MANIFEST_FILE")" > "${MANIFEST_FILE}.sha256"

COMPLIANCE_PCT=$(( SATISFIED_COUNT * 100 / TOTAL_CONTROLS ))
log_message "INFO" "=== SUMMARY: $SATISFIED_COUNT/$TOTAL_CONTROLS controls SATISFIED (${COMPLIANCE_PCT}%) ==="
log_message "INFO" "Evidence manifest checksum (root proof): $MANIFEST_CHECKSUM"

# ---------- Assemble the final HTML compliance report ----------
cat < "$REPORT_FILE"




  

    
NovaTrust Bank

    
Compliance Evidence Package

    
Generated: ${GENERATED_AT}

  

  

    
${SATISFIED_COUNT} of ${TOTAL_CONTROLS} controls SATISFIED (${COMPLIANCE_PCT}%)

    
      ${TABLE_ROWS}
    

      

        Control
        Description
        Status
      

    

      Evidence Manifest Root Checksum (SHA-256): ${MANIFEST_CHECKSUM}

      This checksum cryptographically verifies every evidence file referenced above as of the generation timestamp. Any subsequent alteration to any evidence file changes this value.
    

  




EOF

# ---------- Prune evidence packages older than the retention period ----------
find "$SCRIPT_DIR/$PACKAGE_DIR/.." -maxdepth 1 -type d -mtime +"$(( EVIDENCE_RETENTION_YEARS * 365 ))" -exec rm -rf {} \; 2>/dev/null

log_message "INFO" "Compliance evidence package complete: $PACKAGE_DIR"
exit 0

Every Line, Explained

LineExplanation
: > "$MANIFEST_FILE"The colon : is Bash's no-op builtin; combined with output redirection, this is a compact idiom for "create this file empty, or truncate it if it already exists" — this run's manifest must start completely fresh, never appending to a stale previous run's entries.
Manifest-building loop nested inside the controls loopEvery evidence file referenced by any control gets added to the same shared manifest as the controls loop processes each one — by the end, the manifest represents the complete evidence set backing every control in the report.
MANIFEST_CHECKSUM=$(sha256sum "$MANIFEST_FILE" | awk '{print $1}')This single checksum is the "root" of the entire tamper-evident chain — since the manifest itself lists every individual evidence file's own checksum, this one value indirectly verifies the complete package's integrity.
Both the manifest checksum and the generation timestamp are embedded directly in the HTML reportAn auditor reading the report itself sees the exact proof needed to verify authenticity — no separate document or side-channel required to find this critical evidentiary information.
find ... -mtime +"$(( EVIDENCE_RETENTION_YEARS * 365 ))"Reinforces Project 5's exact pruning technique, but with a retention period measured in years (converted to an approximate day count) rather than the days-scale retention appropriate for routine operational logs.

📄 README.md

README.md
# NovaTrust Bank - Compliance Validation & Evidence Packaging

## Usage
    ./scripts/compliance_validation.sh

## Adding a New Control
Add a line to config/controls_registry.txt:
    CONTROL-ID : Description : evidence/source/path1,evidence/source/path2
Leave the evidence sources empty for a control not yet technically assessed.

## Control Status
    SATISFIED     = every mapped evidence source indicates a passing result
    NOT_SATISFIED = at least one mapped evidence source indicates a failure
    NOT_ASSESSED  = no evidence source mapped yet (honest gap, not a failure)

## Verifying Package Integrity Later
    cd evidence_packages/YYYYMMDD
    sha256sum -c evidence_manifest.txt.sha256    # Verifies the manifest itself
    sha256sum -c evidence_manifest.txt            # Verifies every individual evidence file

## Retention
Evidence packages are retained for EVIDENCE_RETENTION_YEARS (default 7) -
a regulatory retention period, NOT the same as routine operational log rotation.

🔄 What Happens Internally

  1. Every control's evidence sources are read fresh on each run, never cached or assumed from a prior package — each monthly evidence package is a genuinely independent, complete snapshot.
  2. The tamper-evident chain has two distinct layers: individual file checksums inside the manifest, and one checksum of the manifest itself — verifying the outer layer transitively proves the inner layer wasn't altered, without needing to separately communicate hundreds of individual checksums.
  3. A control's evaluation short-circuits on the first failing source, exactly like Project 24's release-gate logic — once any required evidence source fails, the control cannot be SATISFIED regardless of what any other source shows.
  4. The generation timestamp and root checksum are embedded directly in the human-readable report, not hidden in a separate file — an auditor examining the report itself has immediate access to the exact information needed to independently verify its authenticity.

🛡️ Production Best Practices

Distinguish "Not Yet Assessed" From "Failed"

An honest gap in evidence coverage and an actual control failure carry very different meanings — conflating them either overstates risk or understates it.

Layer Tamper-Evidence, Don't Rely on One File Alone

Individual evidence checksums plus one root manifest checksum together prove the integrity of an entire package with just one number to communicate or record externally.

Require All Sources for a Multi-Source Control

A control backed by several evidence sources should only be considered satisfied if every one of them genuinely passes — partial evidence isn't sufficient evidence.

Timestamp Evidence Explicitly and Visibly

An auditor needs to know precisely when a snapshot was taken — embed this prominently in the report itself, not buried in a filename alone.

Give Compliance Evidence Its Own Retention Policy

Regulatory retention requirements are typically far longer than routine operational log rotation — never apply the same short-term retention policy to both.

Treat the Controls Registry as a Joint Ownership Artifact

Mapping technical checks to regulatory language is a decision requiring Compliance/Legal sign-off, not a purely engineering judgment call.

🎤 Interview Preparation

Scenario: An auditor asks how they can independently verify this evidence package hasn't been altered since generation. What do you tell them?
Point them to the root manifest checksum embedded in the report — if they have (or are given) that value through a trusted separate channel, they can re-run sha256sum -c against the manifest and every individual evidence file in the package; any alteration anywhere, down to a single byte in any evidence file, would produce a checksum mismatch, providing cryptographic (not just procedural) proof of integrity.
Production Support: A control that was SATISFIED last month now shows NOT_ASSESSED this month, even though nothing about the underlying systems changed. What's the likely cause?
The evidence source path in the registry likely became invalid — perhaps the underlying project's log directory was moved, renamed, or a typo was introduced in a registry edit — causing source_indicates_pass's existence check to fail and the control to fall back to the "no valid source" NOT_ASSESSED classification rather than genuinely reflecting the real control status.
Shell Scripting: Why does evaluate_control use local IFS=',' scoped specifically to the array-assignment line, rather than changing IFS globally for the whole script?
Changing IFS globally would affect every other word-splitting operation throughout the rest of the script (including other loops reading colon- or space-delimited data), almost certainly breaking unrelated parsing elsewhere; scoping the change to a single local variable for just the one line that needs comma-splitting keeps the effect completely contained.
Troubleshooting: The manifest verification (sha256sum -c) fails for every file, even ones you're confident weren't touched. What's the most likely explanation?
The verification is very likely being run from a different working directory than where the manifest's paths were originally recorded — since the manifest stores paths as written at generation time, running sha256sum -c from anywhere other than the exact original relative-path context will report every file as unreadable or mismatched, not because the files actually changed.
Manager Round: Why does this project deliberately leave one control (AU-6) with no mapped evidence source, rather than mapping it to something imperfect just to show a SATISFIED status?
Honestly reporting a genuine gap as NOT_ASSESSED is far more valuable and credible to both internal leadership and an external auditor than forcing a technically-imperfect mapping just to avoid an uncomfortable gap in the report — a compliance program's credibility depends on its gaps being visible and tracked, not hidden behind a superficially complete-looking report.
Architecture: Why does this project's evaluate_control function require ALL evidence sources to pass, rather than, say, a majority or any-one-of pattern?
A regulatory control typically represents a specific, complete requirement — if a control genuinely needs both proper account lifecycle management AND least-privilege enforcement to be truly met, partial coverage (one passing, one failing) means the actual underlying risk the control exists to address is still present; "all must pass" accurately reflects that a control is either genuinely met or it isn't, with no meaningful middle ground.
Best Practice: Why generate a NEW, completely fresh manifest on every run (via the : > "$MANIFEST_FILE" truncation) rather than appending to or updating a persistent manifest over time?
Each compliance evidence package is meant to represent one complete, independent, dated snapshot — an auditor examining the August package needs that package's own manifest to reflect exactly and only August's evidence state, not an accumulated or partially-updated history mixed in with other periods' data.
Real Incident Discussion: What's the single most direct design decision in this project that would have prevented the original evidence-rejection problem during the real regulatory audit?
Embedding a cryptographic root checksum and an explicit generation timestamp directly in the evidence report itself — the auditor's original objection was specifically the inability to verify the manually-assembled evidence's authenticity and dating; this project's tamper-evident manifest chain directly and completely answers exactly that objection with mathematical proof rather than a manual assurance.
